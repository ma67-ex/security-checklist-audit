Frontend security

5 items

Use HTTPS everywhere

Needs review

What it checks: whether your site encrypts data between visitors' browsers and your server.

Why it matters: without it, anyone on the same network as your visitor can read or change everything they send, including passwords and form data.

Evidence
No http:// links found in the code. Nothing confirms HTTPS is
forced either. This is set at the hosting layer (GitHub Pages, Vercel,
and the like), not in this repo. Check your host's settings to confirm
HTTPS is forced.

Input validation and sanitization

Needs review

What it checks: whether text visitors type in gets checked before your site trusts it.

Why it matters: unchecked input is how attackers slip in malicious scripts (called XSS) that run in other visitors' browsers.

Evidence
index.html:272  <input id="searchInput" placeholder="Search symbol or company">

One input field. It filters data the page already fetched, on the client
side. No dangerous sink (innerHTML, eval) shows up anywhere in the scan,
so the risk is low given the small surface. There is no explicit
validation either, so this is not a confirmed pass.

Don't store sensitive data in the browser

Secure

What it checks: whether passwords, tokens, or personal info end up sitting in the browser, where anyone with dev tools open can read them.

Why it matters: browser storage is not private. A shared computer or a simple "view source" can expose it.

Evidence
No localStorage or sessionStorage use found anywhere in the scanned
files. There is nothing sensitive held on the client side.

CSRF protection

Not applicable

What it checks: whether actions on your site can be triggered by a link on some other website your visitor has open.

Why it matters: without protection, a malicious page can quietly act as your logged-in visitor.

Evidence
No forms and no state-changing requests exist in this app. It is a
read-only stock lookup, so there is nothing for CSRF to attack.

Never expose API keys in frontend

Not applicable

What it checks: whether secret credentials for other services end up baked into code visitors' browsers download.

Why it matters: anything sent to the browser can be pulled back out by anyone. A leaked key can mean someone else runs up your bill.

Evidence
No API key patterns found. The app calls free, public stock-data APIs
that do not need a key.

Authorization checks

Not applicable

What it checks: whether the app confirms a specific user is allowed to see or change a specific thing, not just that they are logged in.

Why it matters: without it, anyone can view or delete someone else's data by changing an id in the URL. This bug is called IDOR.

Evidence
No user-owned resources exist in this app for authorization to apply to.

Legal & compliance

Privacy policy

Missing

What it is: a page that tells visitors what personal data you collect and what you do with it.

Why it matters: it is legally required almost anywhere you collect personal data (GDPR, CCPA, and similar). This app shows no forms, analytics, accounts, or cookies, so it is probably not required at its current scope. Re-check if you add analytics or ads later.

Terms of service

Missing

What it is: the rules visitors and users agree to by using your site.

Why it matters: it is not tied to data collection. It is a common baseline for public sites. Weigh it against how commercial the project actually is.

Accessibility statement

Missing

What it is: a page describing how your site supports people with disabilities, such as screen reader and keyboard support. Some people call this the disability page.

Why it matters: it is expected for public US sites under the ADA. The statement means little unless the site is actually usable with a keyboard and screen reader, so check that separately. This scan cannot verify it.

This is a heuristic first-pass scan. It matches patterns with regular expressions, with no AST, no data-flow analysis, and no live CVE database. It is good for catching obvious gaps before a push. It is not a substitute for CodeQL, Semgrep, or a professional pentest. The legal-pages section is presence-detection, not legal advice. Confirm real requirements with a lawyer for anything beyond a personal project.
